11Agricultural impact

What changes when a field is measured at plant scale.

The value of the project is not the rover itself — it is the resolution of the data. Once every cell has a reading and a crop assignment, the question shifts from “how much should this field receive” to “what does this plant need”.

Paired-column intercropping

Two crops, two requirement sets, one continuous mission.

The rover resolves the profile from the column it is standing in, so treatment follows the crop rather than the field boundary.

Demonstrated

01

Working in the prototype

  • Autonomous serpentine grid navigation
  • IMU-based movement without live GPS
  • Cell-by-cell soil measurement
  • Multiple configurable crop profiles
  • Four operating modes
  • Conditional fertilizer application
  • Blynk telemetry to a farmer dashboard

Expected by design

02

Reasoned, not yet measured

  • Fewer unnecessary fertilizer applications
  • More informed nutrient management decisions
  • Lower potential for nutrient runoff
  • Clearer visibility of soil conditions between lab samples
  • Practical support for intercropped fields
  • Less dependence on manual sampling labour

Requires field trials

03

Open questions, stated honestly

  • Any quantified reduction in fertilizer use
  • Effects on yield across a full season
  • Measured change in nutrient runoff
  • Water savings — not claimed at all
  • Economic return per hectare
  • Multi-season reliability and service life

No agricultural trial results are presented on this site. The second and third columns describe design intent and the questions that only independent field work can answer.
